The best WhatsApp bot for business: 7 criteria before you choose

Many business owners search for "the best WhatsApp bot", but what's best for a clinic isn't best for a store or a real estate office. Instead of a list of names, here are seven criteria to measure any bot against before you pay.

iDX team · 14 November 2026

1. Does it run on the official WhatsApp platform?

The first and most important question. A bot your business relies on should run on Meta's official WhatsApp Business Platform (WhatsApp Business API). Unofficial auto-reply tools that run on the regular WhatsApp app break WhatsApp's terms and can get your number banned at any time.

2. Does it speak the way your customers do?

In Saudi Arabia, customers write in Saudi dialect, sometimes with typos, and sometimes in English. Ask to try the bot yourself and write to it the way your customers actually write, not the way marketing demos do.

3. Does it hand customers over to a person when needed?

The worst experience is a customer stuck with a bot that doesn't understand them. A good bot knows when to stop and hands the conversation to the right employee with a summary, so the customer doesn't have to repeat themselves.

4. Does it connect to your systems?

An isolated bot only replies. A bot connected to your booking system, CRM or spreadsheets books, records and updates on its own. Ask which systems it connects to and how data moves.

5. Does it give you reports with numbers?

You need to know how many messages came in, how many became bookings or orders, how long replies took, and which staff fell behind. Without numbers, you won't know whether the bot is worth what you pay.

6. What's the full cost?

The cost has two parts: the bot or provider subscription, and Meta's message fees. Under Meta's pricing in effect since October 2026, each number gets 1,000 free reply messages a month inside the customer service window, after which each message is charged, and template messages such as marketing and notifications are charged too. Ask the provider to explain both parts before you sign.

7. Who builds it and who runs it?

There are two kinds of providers: platforms you subscribe to and build the bot in yourself, and companies that build it around your procedures and run it for you. A platform is usually cheaper but needs your time and know-how; a company suits you better if you don't have a team to dedicate to it.

Watch Meta's new policy

Since 15 January 2026, Meta's WhatsApp Business Platform terms prohibit general-purpose assistants that answer any question on any topic, such as ChatGPT. Bots that serve the business, such as customer service, bookings and FAQs, remain allowed. Make sure the bot you choose is built to serve your business.

Questions to ask any provider

  • Is the bot on the official WhatsApp platform?
  • Can I try it before paying?
  • Who writes the replies and flows, me or you?
  • What Meta fees should I expect for my message volume?
  • What happens if I want to stop or switch?
